Why can't you hold your breath for longer? Because the burning urge to breathe is not about oxygen. It is about carbon dioxide, and a freediver learns to wait that alarm out for almost twelve minutes. We follow the alarm from your brainstem to your diaphragm, meet seals, whales and the dive reflex, and explain the deadly trap of shallow water blackout.

In this video:
- The burning feeling
- The wrong alarm
- The built-in diving machine
- The trap that kills

SAFETY: never hold your breath in or near water alone, never hyperventilate before swimming underwater, and never push through the alarm in a pool, lake or sea. Do not attempt records. This video is education, not training advice.

FAQ
How long can a person hold their breath? Most untrained people last about a minute.
Why do you have to breathe? Rising carbon dioxide, not falling oxygen, triggers the urge.
What is the record? About 11.5-12 minutes on plain air and over 24 minutes after breathing pure oxygen (Guinness).
What is the mammalian dive reflex? Cold water on the face slows the heart and reroutes blood to heart and brain.
What is shallow water blackout? Loss of consciousness from low oxygen, often after hyperventilating, with no warning.
