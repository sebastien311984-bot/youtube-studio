On the night of August 21, 1986, Lake Nyos in Cameroon released a huge cloud of carbon dioxide that killed about 1,700 people and some 3,500 cattle in their sleep. How does a lake do that, and could it happen again?

In this video:
- The night Lake Nyos erupted
- The morning after
- The mystery: Monoun, 1984, and the trigger
- The science: a lake like a soda bottle
- Could it happen again? Degassing pipes and Lake Kivu

FAQ
What is a limnic eruption? A sudden release of dissolved gas, mostly CO2, from a deep lake.
Why did people die? Carbon dioxide is heavier than air, flowed into villages and displaced breathable air.
What triggered Nyos? Unproven: a landslide, cold rain or wind, or a small volcanic event are the main candidates.
Can it happen again? Pipes now release gas at Nyos and Monoun. Kivu holds far more gas but is considered much less primed.
