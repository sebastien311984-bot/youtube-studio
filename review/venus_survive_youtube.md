Could you survive on Venus? We fall from 100 km to the surface: sulfuric acid clouds, the one layer at about 50 km where pressure is Earth-like, then crushing pressure (about 90 atmospheres) and about 465 °C. Plus the Soviet lander Venera 13, which sent some of the first colour photos of the surface and worked about 127 minutes in 1982.

In this video:
- What do you see at 100 km?
- What are the clouds made of?
- Where is Venus like Earth?
- What happens below?
- What is the surface like?
- Are there volcanoes?
- How do we see through the clouds?
- Venera 13: the 127-minute mission

FAQ
Why is Venus hotter than Mercury? Its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat in a runaway greenhouse.
How hot is the surface? About 465 °C.
How much pressure? About 90 times Earth's.
Who landed on Venus? Soviet Venera probes; Venera 13 worked about 127 minutes in 1982.
